Welcome to the ERP

This manual explains what each role can see, what each role can do, and where work passes from one team to another.

The ERP is designed around job responsibility. Your menu is intentionally different from another user’s menu. Seeing fewer screens is a security feature—not missing functionality.

✓
Simple rule
Your access is the combination of your active role, selected company and plant, action permissions, and the current status of the record.

How to use this guide

  • New users: read pages 3–5, then go to your role section.
  • Operations: use sections 4–6 for daily work and Finance hand-offs.
  • Finance: use sections 7–9 for approvals, posting, reconciliation and review.
  • Administrators: use sections 12–14 before creating users or custom roles.
  • Auditors and managers: use the complete access and action appendices.

02 · Access model

Why two people see different things

The system applies several controls together. A visible button is not the only security boundary; the API repeats every check.

1 · IdentityNamed accountThe user must be active, verified where required, and signed in.
2 · AssignmentCompany + plantAn active, in-date role assignment is required for the selected workplace.
3 · ScreenCan I open it?Screen access decides whether the item appears in the menu and whether its API can be read.
4 · ActionCan I change it?Actions such as Create, Approve, Post, Pay or Close require their own permission.
5 · WorkflowIs it valid now?Status, value bands, maker-checker rules, version and data checks can still block the action.

View does not mean edit

A Finance Manager can view Production Costs but cannot calculate them. An Operations Manager can view Maintenance and create work; Finance can complete the accounting hand-off.

Multiple roles combine

If a user has two valid assignments in the same workplace, their effective permissions are combined. Use this sparingly and review the resulting segregation of duties.

Partner access is narrower

A Partner User needs both the Partner role and explicit tenant entitlements. They never inherit internal approvals.

When access is working correctly

  • The sidebar contains only assigned business screens.
  • Unauthorised direct links return the user to safe work.
  • Protected APIs return “forbidden” even if someone constructs a request manually.
  • Buttons appear only for valid permissions and record states.

When to contact an administrator

  • Your company or plant is missing.
  • Your role has expired or is inactive.
  • A required screen is absent for an approved job duty.
  • A colleague left and their assignments or sessions must be revoked.

04 · Operations Manager

A dependable daily routine

Use “My work” as the starting point; use registers to investigate and complete the underlying record.

1

Confirm the workplace

Check the company, plant and “Operations Manager” label in the top bar before entering any quantity or making a status change.

2

Review My work

Check open, assigned, pending and overdue items. Claim work when ownership is required; open the source record before deciding.

3

Clear physical exceptions first

Prioritise held stock, incoming-quality decisions, shortages, production holds, overdue stages, loading problems and safety alerts.

4

Process planned work

Continue approved purchasing, stock movements, released schedules, production, quality and dispatch in their expected sequence.

5

Complete evidence

Use meaningful reasons, references and attachments. Never use a status change merely to remove an item from the queue.

6

Hand off and reconcile

Confirm that posted quantities and completed records are visible to Finance. Escalate discrepancies instead of creating compensating data.

04 · Operations Manager

Purchase-to-receipt workflow

Operations creates and executes the request; Finance independently approves the requisition and later owns invoice/payment controls.

Operations1 · Purchase requisitionCreate item lines, purpose, need-by date and estimate; submit for approval.
Finance2 · Independent approvalReview value, purpose, policy and authority; approve or return with a reason.
Operations3 · Supplier quotationIssue request, capture supplier responses and award with evidence.
Operations4 · Purchase orderCreate from the award, amend if controlled, then issue.
Operations5 · Receive + inspectRecord arrival, partial receipt and incoming quality decision.
Finance6 · Match + payUse accepted quantities for three-way match, approval and settlement.

Operations checklist

  • Use the correct supplier, item, quantity and unit.
  • Do not source an unapproved requisition.
  • Capture all quote terms before award.
  • Receive only against an issued purchase order.
  • Record lot and held location accurately.
  • Return only rejected, available stock to the supplier.

Stop and investigate when

  • Estimate, quote and order do not agree.
  • Received quantity exceeds remaining order quantity.
  • The lot, owner or unit is unclear.
  • Accepted plus rejected does not equal inspected quantity.
  • A version conflict says someone changed the record.
  • Finance reports a match exception.

04 · Operations Manager

Inventory and inbound control

Inventory is a ledger of accountable movements—not a number to overwrite. Always use the operation that describes what physically happened.

Stock overview

Review physical, reserved, blocked and available quantities by item, owner, lot, location, quality and expiry.

Actions: manage owners/lots; reserve and release.

Issues & returns

Use for authorised consumption or return of stock when the movement is not already created by a dedicated workflow.

Actions: create, edit, post, cancel.

Stock transfers

Move stock between controlled plant locations while preserving owner, lot, quality and movement evidence.

Actions: create, edit, post, cancel.

Stock counts

Record the counted quantity and reason. Posting creates the controlled adjustment; it does not rewrite history.

Actions: create, edit, post, cancel.

Expiry & disposal

Identify expiring stock and dispose only through the governed record with reason and outcome location.

Actions: create, edit, post, cancel.

Incoming quality

Split the inspected held quantity into accepted and rejected quantities. The total must reconcile exactly.

Action: complete inspection.

!
Never correct stock by guessing.
If the wrong item, unit, lot, owner, location or quality status was posted, stop and follow the approved correction/reversal process. Preserve the original audit trail.

Before posting a movement

  • Correct workplace and location
  • Correct item/SKU and unit
  • Correct owner and lot
  • Enough available quantity
  • Reason and source reference entered

After posting

  • Confirm the movement number/status
  • Check resulting stock availability
  • Confirm linked source record
  • Resolve any work-queue exception
  • Notify the next owner when required
04 · Operations Manager

Plan and execute production

Planning creates controlled snapshots; execution consumes the released plan and records actual material, time, output and quality.

PlanDemand planningCreate time-phased manufactured-product demand and release it.
MaterialsMaterial planningRun recipe explosion and stock netting; resolve shortages.
CapacityProduction scheduleSchedule routes against work-centre capacity; reserve materials.
BatchProduction orderCreate, release and issue the exact reserved material.
ActualsStages + outputStart/complete in sequence; record good yield, loss and rework.
ReleaseQuality + packingComplete tests and holds before packing released output.

Release gates

  • An effective recipe and active production route exist.
  • Material shortages and capacity overloads are resolved.
  • Reserved material is available by lot and expiry.
  • Stages are completed in route order.
  • Good + loss + unresolved rework reconciles to actual output.
  • Required lab results and food-safety holds are clear.

What the system preserves

  • Recipe, material and route snapshot used by the order.
  • Exact reservations and stock issues.
  • Stage timestamps and actual duration.
  • Yield, loss, rework and resolution history.
  • Specification results, deviations and holds.
  • Finished-lot genealogy, coding and cost evidence.
i
Cancel carefully.
Cancelling a released schedule or order can return reservations, but only while downstream execution allows it. Read the displayed reason when cancellation is blocked.

05 · Finance Manager

A dependable daily routine

Start with approvals and exceptions, then move through matching, posting, reconciliation and review.

1

Confirm scope

Verify company, plant and Finance Manager in the top bar. A report or journal in the wrong scope is not a harmless mistake.

2

Review My work

Open approval and exception items from their source. Claim only when ownership is required and leave a decision reason.

3

Resolve matches

Compare invoice lines to purchase order, QC-accepted receipt quantity, price and tax before approving.

4

Post controlled entries

Confirm maker-checker separation, balanced lines, open period, correct accounts and immutable source evidence.

5

Reconcile

Allocate receipts/payments exactly, use unique bank references, and investigate unallocated or unmatched amounts.

6

Review and report

Run controlled reports with an explicit cutoff. Use the stored snapshot/export rather than rebuilding totals manually.

Approval questions

  • Is the request from another user?
  • Is it within direct or delegated authority?
  • Is the purpose complete and supported?
  • Do value, supplier/customer and terms make sense?
  • Is the source version unchanged?
  • Is a rejection reason specific enough to correct?

End-of-day questions

  • Any approved but unposted items?
  • Any payment or receipt not reconciled?
  • Any journal draft or imbalance?
  • Any nearing period-close dependency?
  • Any failed export or unacknowledged file?
  • Any overdue Finance work item?
!
Never approve because the requester is waiting.
If evidence is incomplete, reject or return it with a useful reason. Approval is an accountable control, not a queue-clearing action.

i

Payables and the general ledger

Create or invite a user

Invitation is the preferred route because the person sets their own secret. Direct account creation is intended for controlled local/UAT use.

1

Choose the workplace

2

3

4

5

The user and initial plant assignment are created together. Deliver invitation links only through the approved channel.

6

Ask the user to sign in, choose the intended workplace and confirm their menu. Do not ask them to share their password.

7

8

Deactivate assignments/accounts when duties change; resend/revoke invitations, send verification and revoke device sessions as needed.

!
Changes take effect through the active session context.

08 · ERP Administrator

Permission guardrails

Permissions are powerful, but they are only one layer of the control model.

Screen permission

Allows the menu item and protected read route. Friendly example: View General ledger.

Action permission

Allows a command family. Friendly example: Post journal. It does not bypass status or validation.

Assignment

Binds the role to a user, company and plant, optionally for a fixed date window.

Approval authority

Policy/value bands and delegation decide which submitted records a reviewer may decide.

Record entitlement

Partner users need explicit party-bound entitlement in addition to their role.

Workflow rule

Record status, maker identity, current version, quantity/balance and evidence remain server-authoritative.

Recommended access review

  • Monthly: active administrator and Finance assignments.
  • Quarterly: all roles, custom roles and partner entitlements.
  • Immediately: leaver, transfer, suspected compromise or emergency access.
  • Before go-live/change: maker-checker and negative access tests.

Prefer deactivation over deletion

Deactivate an account, assignment, custom role or permission to stop future access while preserving historical attribution and audit evidence.

Protected system definitions cannot be edited or deactivated in the normal editor.

!
Never rely on a hidden button as security.
The backend must enforce the corresponding screen/action permission. This ERP does so for current routes; new features must follow the same pattern before release.

The standard Administrator has all 69 screen permissions and 272 assigned internal/administrative action permissions. Three external Partner-only commands stay with the Partner User.

Includes all internal role actions

Administrator receives every action listed for Sales, Operations and Finance, plus internal partner-administration and elevated control actions. External claim creation, document upload and receipt acknowledgement remain Partner User commands.

Administration actions

  • Create/update companies and plants.
  • Create/update plant locations.
  • Create/update/invite users; assign roles; manage device sessions.
  • Create/update custom roles and permissions; replace role permission set.
  • Create/update approval rules, delegations and escalations.
  • Open protected audit evidence.
  • Process, retry and quarantine integration work.
  • Manage support cases.

Elevated business controls

  • Manage work-queue assignment/escalation.
  • High-value and escalated purchase/return approvals.

  • Approve/accept multi-plant transfer stages.
  • Administer partner access grants and published documents.

Plain-language glossary

Action permission
Allows a specific command such as Create, Approve, Post, Pay or Close.
Assignment
Connects a user and role to one company/plant, optionally for a date range.
Context / workplace
The company and plant currently selected in the top bar.
FEFO
First-expire, first-out lot selection used to protect shelf life.
Maker-checker
One person prepares; another authorised person approves or posts.
Permission
A named authority used by the backend to allow a screen or action.
Role
A reusable bundle of permissions representing a job responsibility.
Screen permission
Allows a business screen to appear and its protected information to be read.
Version conflict
The record changed after you opened it; refresh before deciding.
